# 3. Dashboard – Stock Take / Warehouse Inventory

### Client's Requirement

The client noticed that the dashboard currently does not have a Stock Take feature.

He wants a dedicated section in the dashboard where staff can perform physical inventory counts of all products available in the warehouse.

The client shared a reference screenshot during the meeting.

### Required Functionality

Add a new dashboard section or tab called:

**Warehouse / Stock Take**

The purpose is to allow staff to count and manage the actual physical stock available across all products.

### How It Should Work

1. Staff navigate to the Warehouse or Stock Take section.
2. The system displays the products available on the website.
3. Staff can enter the actual physical quantity available for each product.
4. Staff can perform stock counts for individual products.
5. Staff can also perform stock counts across the entire product catalog.
6. The system should maintain the inventory count as one centralized warehouse stock pool.

### Important Business Logic

The client specifically clarified that stock take does not need to be allocated to individual branches or stores.

He wants one centralized inventory pool for all products.

For example:

- Product A: 150 units
- Product B: 200 units
- Product C: 75 units
- Product D: 120 units

Staff should be able to enter and update these quantities based on their physical warehouse counts.

The client does not want the complexity of managing separate stock counts for multiple stores or branches.

**Important:** This is a centralized warehouse inventory counting feature, not a branch-based inventory allocation system.

The client also mentioned that he can provide additional clarification and reference material for this feature.

# 7. Age Verification – Important Business Logic Clarification

There was a misunderstanding regarding the age verification process.

The previous project discussion suggested that when a customer places their first order, the brand staff or platform team would manually verify whether the customer is 18 years or older before allowing the order to proceed.

The client explicitly rejected this approach.

### Client's Confirmed Requirement

The client wants a simple age verification process.

**Step 1 – Age Verification Popup**

Display an age verification popup to website visitors.

**Step 2 – Checkout Confirmation**

At the end of the purchasing process, customers must tick an age confirmation checkbox on the checkout page.

**Step 3 – Order Placement**

The customer can proceed with the order after confirming the age requirement.

### What Is NOT Required

- No manual age verification by staff.
- No first-order customer verification workflow.
- No staff approval before an order can be placed.
- No order cancellation based on a manual verification process.

The client explicitly confirmed that the popup and checkout checkbox are sufficient for the requested workflow.

**Important:** Remove the previously discussed manual age verification functionality if it has been implemented.

Follow the client's previously shared checkout page reference for the appearance and placement of the checkbox.
